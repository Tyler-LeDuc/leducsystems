Weekly timesheet

Open Saguaro Hours from Apps in the Jira sidebar (under Your apps). Everyone sees the My timesheet and Reports tabs. Team leads, backup approvers and Jira administrators also see Approvals.

The status row shows your timesheet period: weekly, or monthly if your team uses monthly timesheets (periods-and-approvals.md).

Your timers

When you have a timer (started on an issue's Log work panel), it is listed above the status row: running, paused or stopped, the time counted, the issue and its summary. Pause, Resume and Stop and log work as on the panel, and Stop and log opens the same prefilled worklog form (log-work-panel.md). After the worklog is saved the grid reloads with it. The list is read again when you come back to the tab and every 30 seconds, so a timer started in another tab or on another computer shows up.

The grid

  • Rows are issues. Columns are Monday to Sunday of the shown week. Today is highlighted and weekends are shaded.
  • The header shows your name and Jira time zone. Prev, Next, This week and Refresh move and reload the view.
  • Row totals, day totals and a week total are shown.
  • A small green $ in a cell means at least one worklog in it is marked billable.

How hours are shown

Everywhere in Saguaro Hours (grid, Log work panel, timer, approvals, gadget, reports, exports and reminder emails), time is shown as decimal hours with up to two decimals: 1.5 is 1 hour 30 minutes, 0.75 is 45 minutes, 0.25 is 15 minutes, 0.02 is 1 minute.

  • A cell shows the total of all your worklogs on that issue and day. Hover it for the exact time, for example 3.77 is "3h 46m in 3 worklogs" (3 hours, 45 minutes and a 1-minute timer entry).
  • Two decimals always stand for one exact number of minutes, so typing the shown figure back saves the same time.
  • Messages about one entry add the exact time when the decimals round: "Logged 0.02h (1m) on KAN-8". The timer's Time spent box shows "= 1m" under such a figure.
  • A running timer shows a clock (1:02:05) until you stop it.

Entering time

  • Click a cell and type. Enter or leaving the cell saves. Escape reverts.
  • Accepted forms: 1.5, 1:30, 1h 30m, 90m. Invalid text gets a red border and is not saved.
  • A new worklog starts at 09:00 in your Jira time zone on that day.
  • Emptying a cell or entering 0 deletes every worklog in that cell.
  • If a cell holds one worklog, its duration changes and its comment is kept. Clearing it deletes that worklog. Neither asks first.
  • A cell that holds several worklogs shows "n entries" under the total. If you type a new total there, the first worklog gets the total and keeps its comment, and the others are deleted with their comments. The grid asks first: a dialog under the cell says how many worklogs will be deleted, that their comments go with them (and how many have one), and that this cannot be undone. Clearing such a cell asks the same way before deleting all of them.
  • Cancel (focused first; Escape or a click outside does the same) writes nothing and puts the old total back in the cell. The confirm button names what it does, for example "Save 4h and delete 2 worklogs".
  • To change one of several worklogs, use the Log work panel on the issue or Jira's Work log tab.

Adding rows

  • Type an issue key in the box below the grid and press Enter or Add row. As you type, the box suggests issues: Jira's issue search, issues whose summary has the words you typed, and, for a key or a project key such as KAN, that project's recent issues. Pick one with the mouse or the arrow keys and Enter. When nothing matches, the box says so.
  • An empty week says how to start: add an issue here, or use Log work on an issue (log-work-panel.md).
  • An exact key, such as PRJ-1, is always the first suggestion when you can browse that issue, followed by keys that start with what you typed (PRJ-10, PRJ-11 and so on). A project key and a dash, such as PRJ-, lists that project's most recently updated issues.
  • Rows added by hand exist only on the page. They are lost when you reload, unless they have a worklog that week. Rows with hours come back by themselves.
  • Remove a row with the remove button when it has no hours.
  • Copy last week adds last week's rows without hours. It is disabled when the whole shown week is locked. Rows you added by hand, or copied, carry across weeks until you close the page.

Required hours

A chip under the status row compares your logged hours with your required hours for the shown week. It reads, for example, "36.5h short of 40h" in amber or "40h met" in green. The chip is always per week, also when periods are monthly.

Your required hours are the site default (the admin's hours per week, Monday to Friday), or the working hours an admin set for you (for example part-time), less holidays and your days off. A holiday or day off is labelled under the date in the grid header, and the chip's tooltip names the week's holidays. See periods and approvals.

Locked days

When a period is submitted or approved, its cells turn grey and read-only, and the add box is hidden if the whole week is locked. See periods and approvals.

Reminder banner

A banner appears for the previous period if it is short of your required hours or unsubmitted. It links to that period. It also shows for periods that are already submitted or approved but short, where you cannot change anything. The wording follows the status (for example, it mentions a rejection).

The banner only shows if you were using time tracking by then, so a new user is not told about a week before they started. At least one of these must be true:

  • The period has hours or a timesheet record.
  • You have a worklog dated on or before the last day of the period.
  • Your first timesheet record is on or before that day.

Notes

  • Your worklogs are real Jira worklogs. You can edit them in Jira too, but not inside a locked period without the app flagging it (post-lock-warnings.md).
  • The grid shows your own worklogs only.
  • Remaining estimate. Worklogs written in Saguaro Hours change the issue's remaining estimate in Jira the way Log work in Jira does (the default): logging 2h lowers it by 2h (not below zero), changing a worklog moves it by the difference, deleting one gives the time back. A Jira administrator can choose "Leave the remaining estimate unchanged" on the admin page instead. This applies to the grid, the Log work panel, the timer and the imports; worklogs logged in Jira itself follow Jira.
  • The grid does not save a cell until the site settings have loaded (a moment after the page opens), since they decide the lock, the billable default and the remaining estimate rule.
