Questions

Which files open?

Emails saved from Outlook as .msg files (dragged from Outlook to a folder, or saved with File, Save As). That includes old and new Outlook versions, emails in any language, meeting requests, and emails forwarded as attachments inside another email. Whole mailboxes (.pst and .ost files) open in the PST Viewer. It does not open .eml files (any mail app opens those).

Is my email uploaded?

No. The .msg file is read by the page in your browser and never sent to my server or anyone else. The only thing that goes to my server is the license check when you save, which sends your license key and nothing about your email.

What is free?

Reading is free for any number of emails: the sender, recipients, date and subject, the whole message, and every attachment, which you can download one at a time. Forwarded emails inside an email open too. Only saving costs money: $19 once unlocks saving as PDF, all attachments as one .zip, and many emails to PDF at once, or $7 for 24 hours. The license is a one-time payment, no subscription. Downloads keep working for as long as I run this service, and for at least 2 years from your purchase; if I ever shut it down, I'll email you 90 days' notice.

Does it open winmail.dat attachments?

Yes. When Outlook sends an email in its Rich Text format, people who do not use Outlook get a winmail.dat attachment instead of the real attachments. Drop the winmail.dat here and the viewer unpacks it: the message and every file inside it, free to read and download one by one.

Is it safe to open an email here?

The message is shown in a sandboxed frame with scripts turned off, so nothing in the email can run. Links open in a new tab. Pictures the email loads from the internet are blocked until you click "Show them", so the sender can't tell you opened it. Attachments are saved as they are: if you don't trust the sender, scan them before you open them.

What does the PDF look like?

Letter pages with a header block (subject, from, to, cc, date and the names of the attachments) and then the message as it looks on screen, with the pictures that are inside the email. Each page is a picture of the email at print quality, so the text in the PDF can't be selected or searched. Pictures that the email loads from the internet are left out. Before paying you can download page 1 free, marked as a sample.

Can I convert many .msg files at once?

Yes. Select or drop up to 500 .msg files together. They open in a list, and you can read each one free. With the $19 license, one click saves every email as its own PDF, all in one .zip.

What about forwarded emails inside an email?

They show in the attachment list. Click Open to read one in the viewer, with its own attachments, and Back to return. Download saves it as its own .msg file.

Does it work on a Mac, iPhone or Chromebook?

Yes. It runs in Safari, Chrome, Edge and Firefox on Mac, Windows, Linux, ChromeOS, iPhone, iPad and Android. On an iPhone, save the .msg attachment to Files first, then choose it here.

Can I make .msg files open here with a double-click?

On Windows and ChromeOS, yes: install the viewer from Chrome or Edge (the install icon in the address bar, or the prompt after your first email opens), and .msg files open in it with a double-click.

Do you have a license for a business or team?

Yes. The Business license is $99 for one year for up to 5 people at one business, and one key unlocks saving in all six viewers: the MSG and PST viewers (Outlook), the MPP Viewer (Microsoft Project), the XER Viewer (Primavera P6), the Visio Viewer and the Access Viewer. It is a one-time payment that does not renew, and checkout gives you an invoice for your records. If it doesn't work for your files within 30 days, I refund you in full.

No. I'm an independent developer and this viewer isn't affiliated with Microsoft. Outlook is a trademark of Microsoft.
