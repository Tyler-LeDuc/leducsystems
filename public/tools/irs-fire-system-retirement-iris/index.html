The IRS FIRE system is closing: how to file 1099s through IRIS

FIRE's last filing day: November 19, 2026, 3 p.m. Eastern. After January 1, 2027, IRIS is the only IRS system for e-filing information returns. Source: irs.gov/fire, updated October 30, 2026.
Read: Correcting FIRE-filed 1099s

FIRE, the IRS system for e-filing 1099s and other information returns as a Publication 1220 file, takes its last filings on November 19, 2026. After that, everything goes through IRIS. This page covers the dates, the ways into IRIS, getting an IRIS TCC step by step, corrections, and how to choose between filing yourself and using a filing service.

The dates

  • November 19, 2026, 3 p.m. Eastern: "Last day to file information returns through the FIRE system."
  • After January 1, 2027: "IRIS will be the only information returns electronic filing system, including current year, prior year, or corrections."

Both quotes are from the IRS FIRE page, irs.gov/fire (updated October 30, 2026). So 1099s for tax year 2026, due in early 2027, go through IRIS, and so do corrections to returns you filed through FIRE in earlier years.

Paper filing is mostly out, too: since 2024, anyone filing 10 or more information returns in a calendar year generally has to e-file them, counting almost all information return types together (IRS, final regulations on e-file for businesses).

Step by step: getting an IRIS TCC

A FIRE TCC doesn't carry over. The IRIS Taxpayer Portal User Guide says TCCs obtained for FIRE (and for AIR, IRIS A2A and PBBA) "cannot be used in the IRIS Taxpayer Portal" (Publication 5717, Rev. 9-2026). The steps, from the IRS application tutorial (Publication 5903, Rev. 12-2024) and Publication 5717:

  1. Gather what you need. The business's EIN, legal name (and DBA), business structure, physical and mailing addresses and phone numbers.
  2. Line up the people. At least two Responsible Officials and two contacts (one of each for a sole proprietorship, an S corporation or a single-member LLC, per Publication 5903), plus up to two Authorized Delegates. Each Responsible Official and Authorized Delegate signs in with their own IRS account (IRIS Application for TCC).
  3. Start the application. A Responsible Official goes to irs.gov/iristcc, chooses "Access IRIS Application for TCC", signs in, picks the Individual profile, then New Application, then IRIS Application for TCC.
  4. Fill in the firm. Business structure, EIN, names, whether you'll take part in the Combined Federal/State Filing program, addresses and phones.
  5. Choose roles and the transmission method. Issuer files your own returns; Transmitter files for others (an accounting firm or payroll service). A TCC is assigned for each role and method you pick. For the portal, the TCC is five characters starting with "D".
  6. Add the other officials, then sign. The first Responsible Official enters the others, including their SSNs or ITINs. Each one creates a PIN, signs and submits. The application isn't complete until every Responsible Official has.
  7. Wait for the TCC. It shows in the application once assigned, and a letter goes by mail to the application's mailing address. Publication 5717 says to contact the Help Desk if the application sits in "Submitted Pending Review" for more than 45 days. That's why waiting until January is risky.

Uploading a CSV in the portal

From the portal's Dashboard, choose Upload CSV with Form Data and download the template for the form type and tax year. Publication 5717's rules: one tax year and one form type per file, up to 250 forms per file, only the IRS's own templates, and a file can hold several payers if your TCC's role is Transmitter.

If your software exports a Publication 1220 file for FIRE, that file won't go into IRIS as it is. Corrections after FIRE

After January 1, 2027, IRIS is the only system for corrections too (irs.gov/fire, quoted above), and Publication 5717 says FIRE "will not be available for submissions (current, prior year, or corrections) once the system shuts down". So a correction to a return you filed through FIRE will also have to go through IRIS. In the portal, Publication 5717 separates two kinds:

  • One-step corrections: a wrong recipient name or TIN, wrong amounts, a wrong code or indicator, or a form that shouldn't have been filed for a recipient (correct it with zero for every amount).
  • Two-step corrections: the wrong form type, such as a 1099-MISC filed instead of a 1099-NEC. Zero out every amount on the wrong form, then file an original on the right form.

A wrong payer name or TIN can't be corrected in IRIS: Publication 5717 says the payer writes a letter to the IRS instead.

Corrections to forms filed through the portal are made in the portal itself: Dashboard, View Submitted Forms, tick the transmission, then Correct/Replace. A rejected submission is replaced the same way, and Publication 5717 says to do it within 60 days of the rejection, or late-filing penalties may apply once the due date has passed.

That path covers forms filed through the portal. A return filed through FIRE won't be listed under View Submitted Forms. The IRS said in October 2026 that the portal will also take corrections to FIRE originals, but hasn't published the steps yet: correcting 1099s filed through FIRE.

Filing it yourself or using a filing service

A filing service is often the better choice. Pick one if:

  • you file a few dozen forms once a year;
  • you don't have an IRIS TCC and don't want to apply for one, or there isn't time (see the 45 days above);
  • you want them to mail recipient copies, file with the states and handle corrections for you.

For scale: on November 4, 2026, TaxBandits listed federal 1099 e-filing at $2.75 per form for the first 10 forms and $1.75 per form for 11 to 100 forms, with lower rates for larger volumes, state filing through the Combined Federal/State program included, and postal mailing to recipients at $1.85 per form (TaxBandits pricing). Prices change, so check before you choose.

Filing yourself through the portal suits you if you already have (or are getting) an IRIS TCC, you file for many payers or clients, and you'd rather not send every payee's SSN to another company or pay per form each year. The portal itself is free. If your data comes out of software as a Publication 1220 file, the converter above costs $49 per payer, or $149 for a firm filing for all its clients, for the season; checking and previewing is free.

A checklist

  1. Now: apply for an IRIS TCC, or pick a filing service.
  2. By November 19, 2026, 3 p.m. Eastern: anything you still want to file through FIRE.
  3. December: get your recipients' details and your software's export ready; try a test conversion or a single portal form.
  4. January: file. Form 1099-NEC is due January 31, which falls on a Sunday in 2027, so the due date moves to the next business day, February 1 (General Instructions for Certain Information Returns).
