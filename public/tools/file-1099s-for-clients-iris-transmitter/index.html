Filing your clients' 1099s through IRIS as a Transmitter

If you filed clients' 1099s through FIRE, the IRIS equivalent is a Taxpayer Portal TCC with the Transmitter role. One TCC covers every client, and one CSV upload can hold forms for several of them. Here is how it works, from IRS Publication 5717 as of November 2026, and what to set up before January.

Issuer or Transmitter

The IRIS Application for TCC asks each firm to pick a role. Publication 5717 (Rev. 9-2026) defines them:

  • Issuer: "a person filing only for their business. The Issuer's EIN must match the EIN on the Issuer's TCC application."
  • Transmitter: "a person filing for their own business and other businesses or multiple businesses".

If you file for clients, you want Transmitter. The IRS "encourages transmitters who file for multiple issuers to submit one application, select the 'transmitter' role, and use the assigned TCC for all issuers", and says "you may transmit files for as many companies as you need to under one TCC." Your clients don't each need a TCC of their own.

Two more things from Publication 5717: the application needs the firm's EIN (it doesn't take an SSN or ITIN for the firm), and a FIRE TCC can't be used in the portal, so you need a new IRIS TCC even if you've filed through FIRE for years.

Applying, in short

  1. Every person who will sign creates an IRS online account first. Publication 5717 says to have all authorized users do this before you add them.
  2. A Responsible Official starts the IRIS Application for TCC at irs.gov, enters the firm's details, picks the Transmitter role and ticks Portal as the transmission method.
  3. Every Responsible Official signs with their PIN. Then you wait: the IRS says to contact its help desk if the application is in Submitted Pending Review for more than 45 days. Still waiting? What to do while your IRIS TCC is pending.

Once approved, the portal TCC is five characters and starts with "D". Full steps: filing 1099s through IRIS after FIRE.

Filing for many clients in the portal

  • CSV uploads can mix payers. Publication 5717: "The file may contain multiple issuers if your IRIS Taxpayer Portal TCC role is a transmitter." Each row of the IRS template carries its own payer name, TIN and address. A file is still one form type and one tax year, up to 250 forms.
  • Submissions are up to 100 forms. After upload, forms go to Unsubmitted Forms, and the Submit button takes at most 100 forms per submission.
  • Saved issuers are for typing forms in. Issuer Management saves up to 25 frequently used issuers, so you can pick a client from a list when you key a form by hand. You don't need it for CSV uploads, where the payer is in each row.
  • Acknowledgements. Each transmission shows a status (Accepted, Accepted with Errors, Partially Accepted, Rejected), and you can download the acknowledgement. Publication 5717 says to allow 7 days for processing.
  • Recipient copies. The portal can produce PDFs of recipient copies, up to 100 forms per download.

Keeping clients apart

A mixed-payer file is allowed, but separate files per client are easier to check, resubmit and answer questions about. A rejected or corrected submission then touches one client, not ten. When the portal isn't enough

At several thousand forms, portal uploads get slow, and IRIS A2A (XML, through software) may suit you better. It needs its own A2A TCC, a communication test and an API Client ID. Comparison: IRIS Taxpayer Portal vs A2A.

The IRS revises these rules each season. Check Publication 5717 and E-file Forms 1099 with IRIS before filing. This page covers the filing process, not tax advice, and I'm not affiliated with the IRS.
