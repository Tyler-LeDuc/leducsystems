Check a Publication 1220 (FIRE) 1099 file

FIRE's last filing day: November 19, 2026, 3 p.m. Eastern. After January 1, 2027, IRIS is the only IRS system for e-filing information returns. Source: irs.gov/fire, updated October 30, 2026.
Read: Filing 1099s after FIRE · Correcting FIRE-filed 1099s

Drop in the fixed-width 1099 file your accounting or payroll software exports in the Publication 1220 format. It's read in your browser and checked record by record: counts, totals, amount codes, TINs and names. Free, and nothing is uploaded.

What it checks

  • Structure. Every record is a known Publication 1220 type, payee (B) records sit under a payer (A) record, and each payer has its end-of-payer (C) record.
  • Totals. The number of payees and each amount total in the C record against the B records actually in the file, the mismatch FIRE used to reject files for.
  • Amounts. Every payment amount is read with its sign, including the old overpunch format for negatives, and shown against the box it belongs in.
  • Payees. Which TINs are SSNs, ITINs, ATINs or EINs, whether a person's name splits cleanly using the name control, and addresses too long for one line.
  • Corrections. Corrected (G or C) records are counted and listed separately.

It reads 1099-NEC, MISC, INT, DIV, R, G, K and S, 1098, 1098-T and W-2G files for tax years 2022 and later, and previews every form.

Why check it now

FIRE's last filing day is November 19, 2026; after that you can't upload this file there to find out what's wrong with it. From the 2027 filing season, 1099s go through IRIS. If your file checks out, the same page turns it into the IRIS Taxpayer Portal CSV files for $49 per payer. Checking stays free.

It runs entirely in your browser. Your file, with its SSNs and TINs, is never uploaded to me or anyone else.

Questions

Is the check really free?

Yes. Checking and previewing any number of files is free, with no sign-up. Only downloading the IRIS CSV files costs money: $49 for one payer for the 2027 filing season.

Is this the IRS?

No. It's a tool I make. The IRS no longer checks Publication 1220 files once FIRE closes, and IRIS takes a different format.

What happens to my file?

Nothing leaves your computer. The checks run in your browser, so the SSNs and TINs in your file are never uploaded.

Is this tax advice?

No. It checks the file's format and arithmetic; it doesn't decide what you should report. Ask your tax professional about what belongs on each form.
