Periods and approvals

Periods

The admin chooses the site's period type, and each team can use its own.

  • Weekly: Monday to Sunday.
  • Monthly: a calendar month.

On the admin page, Period sets the site default. In a team's card, Timesheet period is "Site default", Weekly or Monthly. A person's timesheets use their team's period type; someone in no team uses the site default. A person can be in several teams only if those teams use the same period type: saving a team, or a site default change, that would give someone two period types is refused with a message naming the person and the teams.

Changing a period type does not convert timesheets already submitted under the old type. They stay in the audit trail; new submissions use the new type. Change it between periods.

Required hours

  • Site default: required hours per week, 40 unless the admin changes it (0 to 168), spread evenly over Monday to Friday (40 h is 8 h a weekday). For a month that is the weekly figure divided by 5, times the number of weekdays in the month.
  • Holidays: the admin lists site-wide holidays (date and name) on the admin page. A holiday needs no hours, for everyone, so it lowers the required hours of that week or month. The grid labels the day with the holiday's name.
  • Working hours per person: on the Working hours tab of the admin page, a Jira administrator adds a person and sets their hours for each weekday, Monday to Sunday, for example 4 h a day for a 20 h part-time week, or 8 h on Monday to Wednesday only. The same card takes the person's days off (dates, such as leave), which need no hours either. Remove puts the person back on the site default.
  • Required hours of a period: each day's hours for that person, less holidays and their days off.
  • Where they are used: the shortfall chip and the reminder banner in My timesheet, the Required column and the amber hours in Approvals, the dashboard gadget, and the Required column of a report grouped by user (reports-and-exports.md).
  • Who sees them: people see their own required hours and days off. Approvers and the gadget see each member's required hours as a number only; the dates of someone's days off are shown only on the admin page.

Submit

  1. In My timesheet, the status row shows the period, a status badge, the last action, a shortfall chip and a Submit timesheet button.
  2. Submit stores the period's hours, computed from Jira, with the record. You can submit when the status is Open or Rejected. A rejected sheet shows Resubmit timesheet. You need to be in a team: someone in no team has nobody to approve the sheet, so the button is off with the reason, and the app refuses such a submit however it is sent.
  3. The period is now locked: grid cells are grey and read-only, and the Log work panel refuses those dates.

Short periods

If you logged less than your required hours for the period, Submit first asks: "You are 6.5h short of 40h this week. Submit anyway?" Submit anyway goes ahead; Cancel leaves the period open so you can add the missing time.

A Jira administrator can turn on "Require full hours before submit" on the admin page (General tab, Periods). Then a short period cannot be submitted at all: the page says how many hours are missing, and the app refuses the submit however it is sent, after reading your hours from Jira again. The rule applies to everyone, Jira administrators included. When time is missing for a good reason (leave, sickness), record a day off under Working hours or a holiday: that lowers the required hours. The option is off by default.

Statuses

Open, Submitted, Approved, Rejected. Each change adds an entry to an audit trail with the actor, time, comment and hours. The last 100 entries are kept.

Approve or reject (team leads, backup approvers and Jira administrators)

Open the Approvals tab. A tab count shows how many sheets wait.

  • Pick a team and a period. Prev, Next, Current and Refresh move through periods, in that team's period type. The line under the bar names the period type, the lead and the backup approver.
  • Each member row shows hours (amber if under that member's required hours), the member's required hours for the period, the status and the last action. Expand it for the audit trail.
  • Approve, or Reject with a reason. A rejection needs a comment, and the app refuses one without it.
  • Approve is offered only for a submitted sheet. An open sheet (never submitted) or a rejected one shows no Approve button: the person submits or resubmits it first. The server refuses any other approval too.
  • A submitted or approved sheet can be rejected. An approved sheet can be rejected again.
  • Approve all (n) approves every member's submitted sheet for the period. n is that number, and the button is greyed out at 0. Open, rejected and already approved sheets are left as they are, and the message after it says how many.
  • The lead, the team's backup approver or a Jira administrator can act. The person must be a member of the team.

Backup approver

A team can have one backup approver, picked in the team's card on the admin page. The backup approver sees the team in Approvals, its pending count and the gadget, and can approve and reject exactly like the lead, at any time, so nothing waits while the lead is away and no hand-over is needed. The lead cannot also be the backup.

Approving your own timesheet

By default a lead who is also a member of their team can approve their own submitted timesheet, as can a Jira administrator. To stop that, turn on "Nobody approves their own timesheet" on the admin page. Then nobody (lead, backup approver or Jira administrator) can approve their own sheet: Approvals shows a note instead of Approve on your own row, Approve all leaves it and says so, and the pending count leaves it out. The backup approver, the lead or another Jira administrator approves it. You can still reject your own sheet, which only unlocks it for editing.

Locks

A submitted or approved period is locked in the app. The app enforces it in the grid, the panel and its own stored data. Jira's own Work log tab and API are not blocked by the app. Edits made there are detected and flagged. See post lock warnings.

To unlock, reject the sheet. The user can then edit and resubmit.

Notes

  • Approvals hours come from Jira as the viewer, so only issues the viewer can browse count, and the viewer's time zone buckets the days.
  • If Jira keeps refusing with a rate limit, Approvals shows a message and "n/a" for hours, and disables Approve and Approve all, so no sheet is approved with wrong hours.
  • Reminders are the banner, the pending count and, if a Jira administrator turns them on, emails through Jira's own mail (email-reminders.md).
